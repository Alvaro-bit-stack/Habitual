## Tennis

*Find your rhythm. One rally at a time.*

**Start for about $34–$110** (required gear); upgrade later for $200–$450: Your own graphite racquet, restringing, court shoes, lessons or clinics.

**Safety:** Check the court, warm up gently, keep clear of others and stop if you feel pain.

**Gear**

| Item | Need | Cost | Borrow | Used OK | Free alternative |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Racquet | required | $30–$100 | yes | yes | Many clubs and public courts lend a racquet for a first session. |
| Tennis balls | required | $4–$10 | yes | yes | Whoever you hit with usually has some. |
| Court shoes | recommended | $50–$100 | no | yes | Running shoes work for your first few sessions. |
| Hat and sunscreen | recommended | $10–$25 | no | yes | — |
| Overgrips | optional | $8–$15 | no | yes | — |

**How to start** (Gamify-Life's start and beginner steps, in order)

1. **Welcome to Tennis**: Read overview and choose a first goal (~10 min)
2. **First Racquet & Balls**: Record owned/borrowed racquet and balls (~10 min)
3. **Court Safety & Etiquette**: Complete safety checklist (~10 min) · safety step
4. **Court, Rules & Scoring**: Correctly log a sample game score (~10 min)
5. **Grip & Ready Position**: Demonstrate/confirm ready position for 10 reps (~10 min)
6. **Contact & Ball Tracking**: Self-drop and contact 20 balls under control (~20 min)
7. **Forehand Fundamentals**: Land 10 controlled forehands in court (~20 min)
8. **Backhand Fundamentals**: Land 10 controlled backhands in court (~20 min)
9. **Split Step & Recovery**: Complete three 60-second shadow-footwork rounds (~20 min)
10. **Five-Shot Rally**: Sustain five cooperative shots (~20 min)

**Beginner resources**

- [USTA Tennis 101](https://www.usta.com/en/home/stay-current/midwest/tennis101.html) (checked 2026-09-21)
- [Intuitive Tennis: Beginner forehand and two-handed backhand (video)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kbmsyOGR6Cc) (checked 2026-09-21)

## Cycling / Fixed Gear

*A little farther. A little more freedom.*

**Start for about $285–$840** (required gear); upgrade later for $400–$1,200: Wheels, saddle, clipless pedals and shoes, a drivetrain refresh, a bike fit, tools.

**Safety:** Check air, brakes and chain before every ride. Use a fitted helmet and follow local traffic rules. Fixed-gear road use needs a functional front brake; do not learn brakeless street riding.

**Gear**

| Item | Need | Cost | Borrow | Used OK | Free alternative |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Helmet | required | $35–$80 | no | no | — |
| Bike (fixed or single speed) | required | $250–$700 | no | yes | A used track bike, or converting an old steel road frame, is the cheapest honest path. |
| Front brake | required | $0–$60 | no | yes | Included on most complete bikes; about $25–60 to add if yours has none. |
| U-lock | recommended | $40–$100 | no | yes | — |
| Front and rear lights | recommended | $20–$60 | no | yes | — |
| Multi-tool, pump and spare tube | optional | $50–$105 | yes | yes | Most shops will lend a floor pump. |

**How to start** (Gamify-Life's start and beginner steps, in order)

1. **Welcome to Cycling**: Choose riding goal and environment (~10 min)
2. **Bike Types & Use Cases**: Identify current/preferred bike type (~10 min)
3. **Bike Anatomy**: Identify 10 major parts (~10 min)
4. **Helmet, Visibility & Traffic Safety**: Complete mandatory safety checklist (~10 min) · safety step
5. **Basic Bike Fit**: Record saddle height check and reach comfort (~10 min)
6. **Pre-Ride ABC Check**: Perform air/brakes/chain check (~20 min) · safety step
7. **Starting, Stopping & Steering**: Complete 20-minute low-traffic control session (~20 min)
8. **Controlled Braking**: Complete 10 smooth controlled stops (~20 min)
9. **First Five-Mile Ride**: Log a safe five-mile ride (~20 min)

**Beginner resources**

- [Sheldon Brown: Fixed Gear for the Road](https://www.sheldonbrown.com/fixed.html) (checked 2026-09-21)
- [League of American Bicyclists: Learn to Ride (free course)](https://learn.bikeleague.org/p/learntoride) (checked 2026-09-21)

## Swimming

*Build confidence, one length at a time.*

**Start for about $30–$100** (required gear); upgrade later for $150–$500: Better goggles, a training set, a swim watch, masters club or coached lessons.

**Safety:** Practice with qualified supervision in a swim-safe environment. Never swim alone, hyperventilate or do breath-holding contests. Restrictive knee bands are not a default freestyle correction.

**Gear**

| Item | Need | Cost | Borrow | Used OK | Free alternative |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Swimsuit | required | $20–$45 | no | no | Any snug athletic swimwear you already own. |
| Goggles | required | $10–$30 | no | no | — |
| Pool access | required | $0–$25 | yes | yes | Campus rec centers and many municipal pools are free or included. |
| Swim cap | recommended | $5–$15 | no | yes | — |
| Towel | recommended | $0–$20 | no | yes | One you own. |
| Fins, pull buoy or kickboard | optional | $10–$50 | yes | yes | Most pools have a shared bin. |

**How to start** (Gamify-Life's start and beginner steps, in order)

1. **Welcome to Swimming**: Choose comfort level and pool access (~10 min)
2. **Water Safety & Pool Rules**: Confirm supervised/swim-safe environment (~10 min) · safety step
3. **Suit, Goggles & Cap**: Record available gear; none beyond safe attire required (~10 min)
4. **Water Comfort & Exhaling**: Complete supervised face-in-water exhale drill (~20 min)
5. **Front & Back Float**: Hold relaxed front/back float with supervision (~20 min)
6. **Body Line & Streamline**: Complete 10 controlled wall glides (~10 min)
7. **Flutter Kick from the Hip**: Complete short kick sets without excessive knee bend (~20 min)
8. **Freestyle Pull**: Swim controlled short lengths focusing on pull (~10 min)
9. **Side Breathing**: Complete repeatable side-breathing drill (~20 min)
10. **Continuous 25**: Swim 25 yards/meters continuously and safely (~20 min)

**Beginner resources**

- [Red Cross Adult Swim Lessons](https://www.redcross.org/take-a-class/swimming/swim-lessons/adult-swim-lessons) (checked 2026-09-21)
- [MySwimPro: How to swim freestyle for beginners](https://blog.myswimpro.com/2021/06/04/how-to-swim-freestyle-for-beginners-5-step-guide/) (checked 2026-09-21)

## Journaling

*Make a little space for your thoughts.*

**Start for about $0–$25** (required gear); upgrade later for $50–$150: A hardcover or dot-grid notebook, a fountain pen and ink.

**Gear**

| Item | Need | Cost | Borrow | Used OK | Free alternative |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Notebook | required | $0–$20 | no | yes | Any notebook you own, loose paper, or a free notes app. |
| Pen | required | $0–$5 | no | yes | Any pen in the house. |

**How to start** (Gamify-Life's start and beginner steps, in order)

1. **Why Journal?**: Pick one personal reason to journal (~10 min)
2. **Choose a Notebook & Pen**: Use something already owned or record a simple choice (~10 min)
3. **First Five-Minute Entry**: Write uninterrupted for five minutes (~20 min)
4. **Make Starting Easy**: Choose a visible place and minimum entry size (~10 min)
5. **Daily Log**: Complete three daily logs (~20 min)
6. **Guided Reflection**: Complete three reflection prompts (~20 min)
7. **Gratitude Without Repetition**: Complete three specific gratitude entries (~20 min)
8. **Seven-Day Experiment**: Journal on five of seven days (~20 min)

**Beginner resources**

- [Bullet Journal: How to start a simple bullet journal](https://bulletjournal.com/blogs/bulletjournalist/how-to-start-a-simple-bullet-journal) (checked 2026-09-21)
- [UC Berkeley Greater Good in Action: Expressive writing](https://ggia.berkeley.edu/practice/expressive_writing) (checked 2026-09-21)

## PC Building

*Understand the machine, then build it.*

**Start for about $629–$1,580** (required gear); upgrade later for $500–$1,500: A faster graphics card, more memory, another drive, a monitor and peripherals.

**Safety:** Unplug at the wall and hold the power button before opening anything. Ground yourself against the bare case or use a strap before touching a board, and never open a power supply — it holds charge long after it is unplugged.

**Gear**

| Item | Need | Cost | Borrow | Used OK | Free alternative |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CPU | required | $90–$250 | no | yes | — |
| Motherboard | required | $70–$180 | no | yes | — |
| Memory (16–32 GB) | required | $99–$220 | no | yes | — |
| Graphics card | required | $200–$450 | no | yes | $0 if your processor has integrated graphics. |
| Storage (1 TB NVMe) | required | $70–$120 | no | yes | — |
| Power supply | required | $50–$110 | no | no | — |
| Case | required | $50–$100 | no | yes | — |
| Operating system | required | $0–$140 | no | yes | Linux is free; student licenses often are too. |
| #2 Phillips screwdriver | required | $0–$10 | no | yes | Almost certainly one in the house already. |
| Anti-static strap | recommended | $0–$15 | no | yes | Touch the bare metal case with the power supply switched off, and avoid carpet. |

**How to start** (Gamify-Life's start and beginner steps, in order)

1. **Welcome to PC Building**: Read the overview and name the machine you want to build (~10 min)
2. **Electrical and ESD Safety**: Confirm you can work unplugged, grounded and on a safe surface (~15 min) · safety step
3. **Parts and Tools**: Record the parts and tools you have or can borrow (~20 min)
4. **Check Part Compatibility**: Verify socket, RAM type, wattage and clearances for your list (~30 min)
5. **Bench Test Before Assembly**: Post the system outside the case with the minimum parts (~45 min)

**Beginner resources**

- [PCPartPicker Build Guides](https://pcpartpicker.com/guide/) (checked 2026-09-21)
- [Linus Tech Tips: How to build a PC (video)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BL4DCEp7blY) (checked 2026-09-21)

## Drawing

*Look closely. The hand follows.*

**Start for about $0–$40** (required gear); upgrade later for $100–$400: A quality sketchbook, graphite and charcoal, a drawing board, figure sessions, a tablet.

**Safety:** Keep the wrist neutral and draw from the shoulder for large shapes. Take a short break every twenty to thirty minutes, work with light across the page, and stop if your hand aches.

**Gear**

| Item | Need | Cost | Borrow | Used OK | Free alternative |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pencil or graphite set | required | $0–$12 | no | yes | Any pencil you own. |
| Paper or sketchbook | required | $0–$20 | no | yes | Printer paper, which is what Drawabox itself recommends. |
| Eraser and sharpener | required | $0–$8 | no | yes | — |
| Fineliner pens (0.5 mm) | recommended | $8–$15 | no | yes | — |

**How to start** (Gamify-Life's start and beginner steps, in order)

1. **Welcome to Drawing**: Read the overview and make one mark on paper (~10 min)
2. **Pencil, Paper, Eraser**: Record the drawing materials you already have (~10 min)
3. **Hands, Eyes and Posture**: Set up a position you can hold comfortably and confirm your breaks (~10 min) · safety step
4. **Line Confidence**: Fill a page with deliberate lines, circles and ellipses (~20 min)
5. **Seeing in Basic Shapes**: Break three real objects down into simple shapes (~30 min)

**Beginner resources**

- [Drawabox Lesson 0: Getting started](https://drawabox.com/lesson/0) (checked 2026-09-21)
- [Proko: Drawing Basics (video playlist)](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLtG4P3lq8RHHMNwxuVk0IcGRtPGHi4vN9) (checked 2026-09-21)

## Painting

*Mix it, and see what the colour really is.*

**Start for about $35–$105** (required gear); upgrade later for $150–$400: Artist-grade paint, stretched canvas, an easel, better brushes, classes.

**Safety:** Work where air moves, keep solvents sealed and away from heat, and never pour them down a drain. Do not eat or point a brush with your mouth. Acrylic and watercolour avoid nearly all of this while you learn.

**Gear**

| Item | Need | Cost | Borrow | Used OK | Free alternative |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Acrylic paint starter set | required | $15–$40 | no | yes | — |
| Brush set | required | $10–$30 | no | yes | — |
| Painting surface | required | $10–$25 | no | yes | Heavy paper or primed cardboard for practice. |
| Palette | required | $0–$10 | no | yes | A paper plate or plastic lid. |
| Water jar and rag | required | $0 | no | yes | Any jar and an old shirt. |

**How to start** (Gamify-Life's start and beginner steps, in order)

1. **Welcome to Painting**: Read the overview and choose acrylic, watercolour or oil (~10 min)
2. **Ventilation and Materials Safety**: Confirm your ventilation, storage and clean-up plan (~15 min) · safety step
3. **A Minimal Palette**: Record the paints, brushes and surface you have or can borrow (~15 min)
4. **Mixing What You See**: Mix and record a ten-step value scale and three matched colours (~40 min)
5. **Brush Control**: Fill a sheet with controlled strokes, edges and washes (~30 min)

**Beginner resources**

- [The Art Sherpa: Beginner acrylic painting course](https://theartsherpa.com/BeginnerAcrylicPaintingCourse) (checked 2026-09-21)
- [Will Kemp Art School: Acrylic painting for beginners](https://willkempartschool.com/acrylic-painting/) (checked 2026-09-21)

## Running

*Slower than you think. Further than you expect.*

**Start for about $60–$180** (required gear); upgrade later for $250–$600: A second pair for rotation, a GPS watch, race entries, coaching.

**Safety:** Build distance gradually and take a lighter week roughly every fourth. Run facing traffic where there is no path, wear something reflective in low light, plan water in heat, and treat new sharp pain as a reason to stop.

**Gear**

| Item | Need | Cost | Borrow | Used OK | Free alternative |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Running shoes | required | $60–$130 | no | no | Any athletic shoe you own will cover your first few weeks; replace it before your mileage builds. |
| Athletic clothes | required | $0–$50 | no | yes | Whatever you already exercise in. |
| Moisture-wicking socks | recommended | $10–$20 | no | yes | — |
| Water bottle | recommended | $0–$25 | no | yes | One you own. |
| Reflective vest or headlamp | recommended | $15–$45 | no | yes | Run in daylight. |

**How to start** (Gamify-Life's start and beginner steps, in order)

1. **Welcome to Running**: Read the overview and choose a first route (~10 min)
2. **Load, Traffic and Heat**: Confirm your progression rule, visibility and hydration plan (~15 min) · safety step
3. **Shoes That Fit**: Record the shoes and visibility gear you have or can borrow (~20 min)
4. **Walk-Run Intervals**: Complete a twenty-minute walk-run session (~25 min)
5. **Finding Easy Pace**: Complete a continuous run at conversational pace (~30 min)

**Beginner resources**

- [RRCA 10-week training plan for new runners](https://www.rrca.org/education/for-runners/10-week-training-plan/) (checked 2026-09-21)
- [NHS: Get running with Couch to 5K](https://www.nhs.uk/better-health/get-active/get-running-with-couch-to-5k/) (checked 2026-09-21)

## Volleyball

*Three touches, and everyone is involved.*

**Start for about $25–$70** (required gear); upgrade later for $150–$400: Your own competition ball, court shoes, better pads, league fees.

**Safety:** Warm the shoulders, wrists and ankles before hitting. Set with firm fingers, land on two feet, keep out of the space under the net, and call loudly so nobody collides.

**Gear**

| Item | Need | Cost | Borrow | Used OK | Free alternative |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Indoor volleyball | required | $25–$70 | yes | yes | Every gym, rec league and pickup group has balls. |
| Knee pads | recommended | $15–$35 | no | yes | — |
| Court shoes | recommended | $50–$110 | no | yes | Any clean non-marking athletic shoe for your first sessions. |
| Ankle braces | optional | $25–$50 | no | yes | — |
| Ball pump | optional | $8–$15 | yes | yes | — |

**How to start** (Gamify-Life's start and beginner steps, in order)

1. **Welcome to Volleyball**: Read the overview and find somewhere people play (~10 min)
2. **Warm-Up, Fingers and Ankles**: Confirm your warm-up and landing habits (~15 min) · safety step
3. **Shoes, Pads and a Ball**: Record the kit you have or can borrow (~15 min)
4. **The Forearm Pass**: Complete fifty controlled passes against a wall or partner (~25 min)
5. **Setting Overhead**: Complete fifty controlled sets (~25 min)

**Beginner resources**

- [USA Volleyball: A beginner's guide (PDF)](https://usavolleyball.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Volleyball-Beginners-Guide-by-SportsEngine.pdf) (checked 2026-09-21)

## Reading

*A few pages, most days.*

**Start for about $0–$18** (required gear); upgrade later for $100–$350: An e-reader, an audiobook subscription, a book budget, shelves.

**Safety:** Read with light on the page rather than behind it, hold the book near eye level, and look at something distant every twenty minutes. Dim screens in the evening and prefer paper near bedtime.

**Gear**

| Item | Need | Cost | Borrow | Used OK | Free alternative |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Library card | required | $0 | no | yes | Libby gives you ebooks and audiobooks on the same card, from your phone. |
| A book | required | $0–$18 | yes | yes | The library, Libby, Project Gutenberg or a Little Free Library. |
| Book light | optional | $10–$20 | no | yes | Any lamp. |

**How to start** (Gamify-Life's start and beginner steps, in order)

1. **Welcome to Reading**: Read the overview and choose one book to start (~10 min)
2. **A Library Card**: Record your library access or the books you already own (~15 min)
3. **Eyes, Light and Sleep**: Set up comfortable light and a screen habit you can keep (~10 min) · safety step
4. **A Daily Reading Slot**: Read on five separate days this week (~25 min)
5. **Reading With a Pencil**: Finish a book while keeping brief notes (~60 min)

**Beginner resources**

- [Libby: Getting started with free library ebooks](https://help.libbyapp.com/en-us/6103.htm) (checked 2026-09-21)
- [Book Riot: How to start reading (if you've never liked it)](https://bookriot.com/how-to-start-reading/) (checked 2026-09-21)
